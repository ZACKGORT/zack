Don’t reinvent the wheel just because you can.

Terms · Manifesto · zack.cx/ada

It’s a bit gimmicky — and that’s intentional. ADA is low-key commentary on the state of design: that accessibility, inclusive practice, and following best-in-class models is still the way forward. Meanwhile the sandbox at zack.cx shows where else we can go.

ADA is a deliberate juxtaposition between design’s obligations and design’s possibilities. Three arguments run at once:

1. Accessibility isn’t the enemy of innovation. ADA is a conventional, recognizable editorial interface — familiar information architecture, readable typography, predictable navigation, proven interaction patterns. Best-in-class products converge on certain conventions because they’ve proven useful. Designers shouldn’t abandon those foundations in pursuit of novelty.

2. The sandbox is where experimentation belongs. Floating windows, nested experiences, unconventional navigation, alternative content surfaces, micro-interactions, conceptual products — exploring what digital experience might become without sacrificing the usability principles that make it accessible. Experimentation doesn’t require making everything experimental.

3. The name ADA does double duty. A nod to accessibility legislation and inclusive design. An editorial environment borrowed from a proven publishing model. A reminder that design has responsibilities beyond aesthetic novelty. There’s a little irony in using an intentionally familiar interface to argue about the future of interfaces.

Thesis: The future of design isn’t abandoning what works. It’s understanding why it works well enough to imagine what comes next. Or more pointedly: make sure the wheel rolls for everyone — then see where else you can take it.

One caveat: the accessibility argument has to extend past visual familiarity. Keyboard navigation, screen-reader semantics, focus management, contrast, reduced motion, and responsive behavior matter more than looking like a familiar product. That creates the opportunity: make ADA the most conventionally usable experience in the sandbox, while the sandbox itself stays unconventional.
