The Challenge

Changing behavior is the hardest brief there is. The Ministry needed Ukrainians to voluntarily give up single-use plastic bags — a habit woven into daily life for decades. The budget was zero. The timeline was three months. Environmental messaging has a historic problem: it makes people feel guilty and then tune out. We needed something that made people want to participate.

"Nobody stops scrolling for environmental news —
but a public breakup? That travels fast."
The Idea

We gave Plastic Bag a personality. A social media persona that had overstayed its welcome — clingy, disposable, and finally getting the breakup it deserved. We launched a nationwide "Divorce from Plastic Bag" flash mob, seeded with opinion leaders who publicly posted their breakup stories. The format was inherently shareable: emotionally familiar, visually simple, and easy to replicate.

The Brief

Pepsi is one of those brands that everyone knows and almost no one thinks about. The challenge was making it feel alive on social — not in a cringey, try-hard way, but in a way that fit the way people actually talk about moments that matter.

"Some things are just better together. The missing piece? Always Pepsi."
The Strategy

The insight was human and obvious once we said it out loud: there are pairings in life that feel inevitable. Movies and popcorn. Summer and friends. Goals and celebrations. Pepsi didn't need to force itself in — it just needed to show up in the right moments, named as the natural constant in any good combination. We built a content system around this pairing logic: formats, recurring themes, and a tone that was warm without being saccharine.

The Work

Some of the most complex content work happens on brands where everyone has an opinion and no one can agree on what it should sound like. Across FMCG, beauty, luxury, and pharma, I developed seasonal content strategies, wrote all creative, briefed designers and photographers, and often attended — or led — production days on set.

"From brief to posted — every decision in between."
Approach

The work that sticks is built on a clear point of view about what a brand sounds like when it's at its best — and a lot of discipline about not drifting from it. That means the strategy, the captions, the shoot mood board, and the briefing doc all speak the same language. When they don't, the content breaks down somewhere between the brief and the post.
