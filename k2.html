No corners. No wrong way up.

Every phone you’ve owned has a top, a bottom, and four corners that find your pocket lining. A circle has no such opinions. At 146 mm across and 5.8 mm per half, it sits in your palm like something you found, not something you were issued. One unbroken ring of polished titanium runs the whole way round, because the edge is the only line it needs.

Look into it, not at it.

Under the glass sits a single curved lens, and the picture lives behind it, not on it. Tilt the phone and the world inside tilts back: a planet turning in the dark, parallax and all. Edge to edge there is nothing to crop around, because there are no corners to crop. Your wallpaper has never had a job before. It does now.

Remember typing?

Somewhere between the first touchscreen and your thousandth autocorrect, we all agreed to stop typing and start approximating. Slide the lower face away and a full QWERTY keyboard is simply there: real rows, rounded keys, a track that glides and then stops exactly where it should. No prediction bar. No second-guessing. Close it and the phone is a smooth disc again, as if nothing had happened.
